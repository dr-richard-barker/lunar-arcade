FIELD MANUAL

GRAVITY, ARCS AND ROBOT DOGS

The drill

Teams of robot dogs take turns. On your turn, walk, jump, pick a tool from the kit and fire. Your dog's mag-paws grip whatever it stands on — floor, wall or ceiling — so changing gravity never knocks a standing dog over. Gravity acts on everything in flight: shots, debris, and any dog that jumps or is blasted off its feet. Last team with a dog standing wins.

The base's gravity plating is fiction: no real moon base can do this. Everything else here is ordinary physics, worked out with the real numbers.

Why the same shot lands somewhere else

The arena is airless on every setting, so a shot feels nothing but gravity and flies a perfect parabola. On flat ground, a shot launched at speed v and angle θ travels R = v²·sin 2θ / g and stays up for T = 2v·sin θ / g.

Same speed, same angle: about six times farther on the Moon. To hit the same spot you need only √(1.62 / 9.81) ≈ 41 % of the Earth launch speed, and the shot hangs in the sky about 2.5 times longer. Hold Shift to fast-forward long flights.

Pointing gravity

The compass sets which way gravity pulls. Turn it a quarter turn and every arc turns with it: a shot that would have landed 40 m down-range now lands 40 m up a wall. Point it across the arena and you can curve a shot round cover; point it at the sky on the surface base and anything that leaves the ground falls away into space.

Jumps, falls and thrusters

A dog jumps at a fixed 3 m/s. It rises about 0.34 m on Earth, 0.9 m on Mars and 2.1 m on the Moon. Falls hurt by impact speed, not height: anything over 6 m/s does damage, which is a 1.8 m drop on Earth, 4.8 m on Mars and 11 m on the Moon (v = √(2gh)).

The Thruster Pack pushes at 6 m/s². Its thrust-to-weight ratio is 6 / g: 3.7 on the Moon, 1.6 on Mars and 0.61 on Earth — where it cannot lift a dog off level ground at all.

No air

No air means no drag and no wind: every grain of dust thrown up by a blast follows the same kind of arc as a shot. It also means no sound. What you hear is your dogs' suit-radio telemetry.

Plants feel gravity too

Plants sense which way is down. Researchers on Earth study what happens when that signal is taken away with machines such as clinostats, which keep turning a plant so that the direction of gravity it experiences never settles [4]. Spinning the compass is the same idea, played for laughs.
